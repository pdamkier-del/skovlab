# Necessary shell and volume bounce conditions

The general spherical equations retain pressure gradients, acceleration and shear. The following inequalities apply to the two selected reduced isotropic sources; they do not establish a collapse trajectory or a sufficient regularity theorem.

For a **noncentral shell areal bounce**, require \(R>0,\ U=D_tR=0\) and

\[
D_tU=\Gamma a_r-\frac{M}{R^2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}(w\rho-q)R>0,
\qquad a_r=-\frac{wD_r\rho-D_rq}{(1+w)\rho-2q}
\]

where the divided formula requires nonzero enthalpy. Spin changes the constrained mass integral, local pressure and acceleration gradients. It is not an independent force chosen to reverse a shell. A general anisotropic/flux model would require its derived radial pressure and momentum equation; these are not free bounce controls.

Spatial curvature constrains \(\Gamma^2=1+U^2-2M/R\). At \(U=0\), \(\Gamma^2=1-2M/R\ge0\). A non-neck turning sphere has opposite-sign null expansions. This is a local trapping statement, not an event-horizon determination.

For a **local material volume minimum**, the volume is proportional to \(BR^2\). Require \(\Theta=H_r+2U/R=0\) and \(D_t\Theta>0\). Raychaudhuri gives

\[
\kappa[4q-(1+3w)\rho]>4\sigma^2-2\nabla_\mu a^\mu,
\qquad
\nabla_\mu a^\mu=D_ra_r+\frac{2\Gamma a_r}{R}+a_r^2.
\]

Shear opposes the volume minimum; acceleration divergence can have either sign. An areal extremum has \(U=0\), but can have nonzero \(H_r\) and volume expansion. A volume extremum can have nonzero \(U\).

For a geodesic region, the necessary volume threshold is \(q>(1+3w)\rho/4+\sigma^2/\kappa\). For \(0\le w<1\) it lies above the positive-enthalpy causal low-spin bound by at least

\[
\frac{(1-w)(1+2w)}4\rho>0.
\]

This conditional obstruction does not exclude pressure-driven spherical turns or another microscopic EC source. In averaged FLRW, shear/acceleration vanish and a turning point must also obey \(\kappa(\rho-q)=3k/a^2\). Only the flat case has \(q=\rho\).

## Regularity checks beyond the turning inequalities

At noncentral positive radius, for the isotropic effective source:

\[
\mathcal R=\kappa[(1-3w)\rho+2q],\qquad
R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}=\kappa^2[(\rho-q)^2+3(w\rho-q)^2],
\]
\[
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=48\left(\frac{M}{R^3}-\frac{\kappa(\rho-q)}6\right)^2.
\]

Bounded ordinary/spin density alone is not sufficient; the mass/areal-radius combination, material Jacobian, metric regularity and continuation also matter. Model B needs bounded coherent spin and covariant derivatives to control full torsionful curvature. Model A's variance scalar does not determine every microscopic curvature realization or its derivative statistics.

Neither condition overcomes a nonhyperbolic material regime or supplies a regular boundary. A complete nonsingular bounce requires actual admissible continuation, finite matter and curvature, no material crossing, and consistent boundary data. No bounce, recollapse, repeated oscillation, event horizon or baby universe is computed or inferred in this theoretical stage.
