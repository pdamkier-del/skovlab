For \(0\le w<1\), \(z=q/\rho\), the causal nondegenerate positive-enthalpy region requires \(0\le z<w(1+w)/2\) with \(w>0\). The interval \(w(1+w)/2<z<(1+w)/2\) has \(c^2<0\). At \((1+w)/2\), the time matrix degenerates; above it, \(c^2>1\). Both model pencils are independently verified. Zero sound speed is a defective endpoint; neither a real repeated root nor a canceled stiff ratio proves regularity.

A positive-density vacuum-pressure interface, \(P=0,\ \rho>0\), has \(q=w\rho\). For \(0<w<1\), this gives \(c^2=-w\), so a causal low-spin fluid patch cannot end there while retaining these assumptions. A zero-density edge needs its own free-boundary/EOS analysis. At \(w=0\), pressure-free matching requires spin zero at the interface; at \(w=1\), the cancellation gives zero enthalpy.

The analytical initial family in the PDF and [boundary report](INITIAL_BOUNDARY.md) verifies that **constraint-satisfying local inhomogeneous data exist**. This is weaker than a well-posed finite star or a demonstrated bounce. It does not impose its initial zero shear on the general equations.

The calculation identifies failures of these selected material closures, not a failure of minimal EC gravity. Geometric EC well-posedness with regular supplied sources and modern causal kinetic spin theories do not supply this fluid's missing dense-matter EOS, moment closure or exterior conditions.

The next theoretical stage must choose and justify the microscopic material model, its high-density susceptibilities/transport, the coupled gauge and characteristic problem, and an admissible boundary. No numerical evolution or plots are part of this stage.
