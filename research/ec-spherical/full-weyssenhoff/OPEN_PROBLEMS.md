# Open problems and explicit implementation blocks

1. **Complete constrained matter action.** The bulk minimal EC variation and the published BHL/Obukhov–Korotky field equations are documented. The original director/material constraints and their independent full variation have not been reproduced from the original 1987 paper. No replacement microscopic action is invented.
2. **Dense-matter EOS.** Constant \(w\) with conserved spin is a conditional material model. Spin-dependent energy, polarization susceptibility, degeneracy physics and the microscopic validity/cutoff scale must be derived for the chosen particles. Gravity alone does not fix them.
3. **Generic spin correlations.** Common-congruence ideal second moments do not repair the acoustic pair. Genuine velocity–spin, shear–spin, acceleration–spin and geometric averages require a hierarchy and constitutive closure. No arbitrary anisotropic pressure, viscosity, relaxation, particle production or torque law is supplied.
4. **Coupled well-posedness.** Real local material roots are necessary, not a full gauge/boundary theorem. Negative acoustic speed squared and enthalpy degeneracy cannot be resolved by grid spacing, a pressure floor or taking \(\sqrt{|c^2|}\). Nonspherical stability is not analyzed by the radial pencil.
5. **Degenerate surfaces and stiff exception.** The zero-sound primitive matrix is defective. A tuned enthalpy-zero solution would need a separate differential-algebraic analysis. Stiff matter has luminal roots away from zero enthalpy, but fixed spin/density ratio and separate energy/boundary questions.
6. **Finite boundary.** The stronger full-curvature matching condition is derived only for a comoving radial coherent branch under stated regularity assumptions. A regular constant-\(w\) compact vacuum edge has not been constructed. Spin-free surfaces must solve transport and metric constraints; no artificial spin window is authorized.
7. **General spinful layers/radiation.** AKP's full component formalism, a surface stress/spin action, distributional regularization and a radiative flux model remain unresolved. The new full-Riemann jump test does not close this general problem.
8. **Global outcomes.** No nonlinear collapse solution or exterior completion is computed. Bounces, repeated oscillations, absence of singularities and event horizons have not been established for Model B.
9. **EC–Dirac comparison.** Derive the chosen microscopic action, conventions and total axial current before reduction; test whether a spherical multiplet cancels torsion; separate classical fields from quantum current correlations. A four-fermion interaction does not by itself establish nonsingular collapse.

The defensible answer is conditional: the selected full ideal fluid **can describe formal inhomogeneous spherical configurations**, but this stage does not establish a causal, physically justified finite high-density bounce evolution. This is not a theorem that every Weyssenhoff formulation or all minimal EC matter must fail.
