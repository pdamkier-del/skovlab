# Recommendation

## Primary: minimal EC–Standard-Model effective theory with QCD-matched many-body matter

This is the most physically defensible foundation for **ordinary interacting fermionic collapse**. The microscopic action includes known gauge/Yukawa interactions and fixes the minimal torsion-induced axial contact coefficient. At nuclear scales its material realization must use nuclear/QCD many-body theory, with leptonic composition and reaction/transport assumptions appropriate to the collapse. It must not be replaced by a free neutron Dirac field extrapolated above its compositeness scale.

This is a **research target**, not an already closed and validated EC collapse EOS. The first tractable question is whether a controlled finite-density calculation can determine

$$
\langle[J_{5,\mathrm{total}}^\mu J_{5,\mathrm{total}\mu}]_{\rm ren}\rangle
$$

and its thermodynamic derivatives, uncertainty and range of validity in one specified state. Match the relevant fundamental, flavor-singlet spin/axial operator into the nuclear theory, including multi-body contributions and renormalization; the neutron mass, an ordinary EOS table, or the measured isovector weak axial charge alone does not fix that matching. At higher density change the degrees of freedom/approximation when justified; do not extrapolate a nuclear model to Planckian quark momenta.

For a first eventual macroscopic test, a **spherical unpolarized bulk state** is the simplest geometry if its computed stress is isotropic and its mean-free-path/relaxation scales justify local hydrodynamics. It can have zero mean axial vector and nonzero correlations. Polarized or strongly magnetized matter instead requires at least axisymmetry and the corresponding anisotropic response. No geometry is derived here.

## Backup: a specified classical minimal ECD–Maxwell benchmark

Use a small number of Dirac species with explicit masses and charges; retain or consistently set the Maxwell field to zero. This gives action-based spin evolution and an algebraic contact interaction with no guessed spin-pressure law. A fixed smooth-background local Cauchy study is simpler than collapse and should precede any coupled experiment.

An **axisymmetric coherent-spin configuration** is a natural eventual choice because polarization selects a direction. A spherical spinor multiplet is possible only after verifying symmetry of its stress and the **total** torsion source. A singlet construction can cancel the classical axial current, removing the very torsion effect intended for study. It is not equivalent to an unpolarized quantum gas with a nonzero connected correlator.

This backup is a mathematical benchmark, not realistic neutron/quark matter: commuting classical spinors do not generate a Fermi sea, nuclear interactions or quantum exchange. It must not be advertised as a realistic stellar bounce prediction.

## What should happen next, before any simulation?

1. Specify species, density/temperature/composition, equilibrium versus neutrino trapping, and polarization. Establish the scales on which local thermodynamics is justified.
2. Fix the minimal EC matching and composite-operator renormalization convention. Compute the axial contact expectation and derivatives in a controlled many-body approximation, including exchange and relevant strong interactions.
3. Check ordinary and corrected susceptibilities, sound modes and microscopic/kinetic validity. A state with a negative compressibility is not cured by the Dirac front cone or numerical resolution.
4. Quantify the EFT error and compare the torsion correction with other omitted effects. Stop the interpretation before that expansion fails.
5. Only then select a closed causal matter evolution and compatible center/boundary problem. Existing spinful junction and global-bounce questions remain unresolved.

Kinetic spin hydrodynamics is a possible later bridge after microscopic matching, rather than the present backup: its dense-matter collision coefficients, susceptibilities, pseudo-gauge/source identification and curved EC coupling have not been supplied. No viscosity, relaxation time, nonminimal coupling or particle-production law is chosen to manufacture stability.

## Direct answer

**Minimal EC coupled to interacting Standard Model fermions, used as an EFT with nuclear/QCD-matched quantum states, is the most realistic foundation; a minimal ECD–Maxwell classical field model is the manageable backup.** The reliable current prediction is that minimal torsion is extraordinarily small at known dense-matter scales. Without a controlled state/correlation calculation and a valid high-energy extension, neither framework establishes a torsion-driven bounce. More microscopic equations improve the question; they do not guarantee the desired outcome.
