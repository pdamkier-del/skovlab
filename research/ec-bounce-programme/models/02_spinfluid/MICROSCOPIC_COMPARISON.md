# Model 2 — microscopic closure gate

**INCONCLUSIVE overall; universal uncorrelated density-squared closure REJECTED WITHIN ASSUMPTIONS.** This is a focused source-comparison cycle, not a new fluid evolution.

Motivation for inspecting Model 2: Model 1 exposes an occupation/correlation obstruction. A spinfluid is useful only if it supplies a justified coarse-graining of those same fermions, not a new freely adjustable negative pressure.

Retained provenance: main's `ec-flrw/collapse/THEORY.md` declares the particular Weyssenhoff averaged closure ρ_eff=ρ−q, P_eff=P−q with q=κS²/4 and S²∝a⁻⁶. `research/ec-spherical/full-weyssenhoff/PHYSICAL_CONSISTENCY.md` and `BOUNCE_ANALYSIS.md` distinguish ideal convective Frenkel spin from quantum Dirac spin, conditional transport, shear/derivative terms and the acoustic obstruction. They were inspected, not rerun. Conservation of an assumed variance does not derive its microscopic value or determine quantum four-point evolution.

New falsification test: match the same massless μ=0 thermal Gaussian Dirac ensemble. Its particle-plus-antiparticle density is nonzero, whereas the independently checked order-G axial Wick expectation is zero. Therefore a universal positive constant q∝κn_total² for this ensemble does not follow from minimal ECD quantum averaging. For charge-asymmetric unpolarized matter the exchange term is instead −D²/2, with species-diagonal dependence. A single scalar spin variance cannot be assigned independently of charge, mass, species and correlations.

This rejects that proposed universal identification, not every Weyssenhoff effective material model. A coarse-grained interacting spinfluid might contain different microscopic correlations, spin–velocity terms and transport coefficients. Those have to be matched from a concrete state; no such alternative closure is derived here. The phenomenological negative-stiff fluid has formal mathematical FLRW bounces, already known. Its homogeneous success does not establish B2, B3 or B6.

Decision: no new spinfluid evolution or radial solver is justified. The specific closure mismatch motivates examining a definite nonequilibrium quantum action as a possible different mechanism. It does not declare the broad microscopic spin-hydrodynamics programme exhausted.
