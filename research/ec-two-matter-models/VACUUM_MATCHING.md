# A finite cloud and a general torsion-free vacuum exterior

## What “vacuum exterior” means

Outside the support of **all** nongravitational sources require

$$
T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad T^{\rm eff}_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
\boxed{G_{\mu\nu}=0}.
$$

The first $T$ denotes torsion; the second denotes stress-energy. Minimal EC makes torsion algebraic, so it has no independently propagating torsion wave in source-free space. The exterior metric can carry Weyl curvature, gravitational radiation, mass and angular momentum. It is not fixed to Schwarzschild or to a spherical geometry.

## A. Dirac fields: a credible local route, with qualifications

Smooth compactly supported **classical Dirac initial fields** are mathematically possible. The nonlinear contact is local and vanishes where the spinors vanish. In a regular hyperbolic Cauchy formulation, matter support has finite causal propagation: this is a route to a matter-free exterior region over a local interval. Initial metric/extrinsic-curvature data must still solve the Einstein constraints; a gravitational field outside the cloud is allowed and is not matter.

This does not justify a fixed timelike material surface. Massive Dirac wave packets can spread within their light cone, and a support front can be null. A positive-energy-only one-particle projection and a general quantum state have different localization properties from arbitrary compact classical Cauchy data. We do not claim exact compact renormalized quantum stress from that classical argument.

If the spinors vanish smoothly in an open collar, their local stress and axial current vanish there; torsion/contortion also vanish. The field and metric equations then reduce to vacuum GR in that collar. This is a **sufficient local source condition**, not a constructed global exterior or a solution of the gravitational constraint-gluing problem. Do not introduce a reflecting wall or an imposed spin window: neither belongs to the given Dirac action.

## B. Kinetic matter: compact initial moments do not finish the EC problem

A smooth occupation matrix/distribution with compact spatial and bounded momentum support can give finite particle, spin and stress moments. Massive classical geodesic characteristics have subluminal speed, so local collisionless transport provides a familiar finite-support mechanism.

For the selected quantum spin theory, higher-gradient currents, self-energies and correlation support must also vanish appropriately. Nonlocal collision shifts can sample both sides of a nominal surface; their near-edge behavior must be derived rather than replaced by a sharp fluid boundary. An arbitrarily thin edge may invalidate the same gradient/quasiparticle expansion used for transport. No dense curved-EC kinetic support/existence theorem has been verified here.

Setting the **mean** spin to zero outside does not prove a spin-free microscopic layer or vanishing contact correlation energy. The two-point distribution alone does not establish the needed four-point boundary correlations. A smooth spin-free, matter-free collar is a conditional sufficient route once a consistent microscopic/kinetic closure and metric data actually produce it.

## Local metric and connection conditions

For a non-null interface with no metric thin shell, let $q_{ab}$ be the induced metric and $\mathcal K^{\rm LC}_{ab}$ its Levi–Civita extrinsic curvature, with a common normal convention. Necessary Darmois conditions are

$$
[q_{ab}]=0,\qquad[\mathcal K^{\rm LC}_{ab}]=0.
$$

Einstein's normal projections also require continuous normal traction/flux; against vacuum a layer-free matter boundary must have $T_{\mu\nu}\nu^\nu=0$. This is a tensor condition, not a copied scalar pressure condition for an arbitrary anisotropic/tilted cloud.

For piecewise bounded contortion $C^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ and a metric matching without distributional LC curvature, its possible singular full curvature is

$$
(\widetilde R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu})_{\rm sing}
=\left(\nu_\mu[C^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}]
-\nu_\nu[C^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}]\right)\delta(\ell).
$$

Here $\ell$ is Gaussian normal distance and the jump is exterior minus interior. Continuous contortion approaching zero removes this delta. For total-antisymmetric Dirac contortion, requiring this full coefficient to vanish against a zero-contortion exterior forces the boundary contortion itself to vanish: antisymmetry cannot leave a nonzero component only in its normal derivative slot. A smooth zero-spinor collar satisfies this stronger regularity requirement.

The assumption of no distributional **full** curvature is stronger than just no metric thin shell. General spinful layers or compensating distributional sources require a specified surface action and an applicable EC junction formalism; none is invented. Null fronts require the corresponding null treatment, not the non-null extrinsic-curvature formula above. A completely smooth source-free collar avoids a singular interface independently of this distinction.

## Two important obstructions

**Electromagnetic exterior.** If Maxwell is included, vanishing fermions does not imply vanishing electromagnetic stress. Gauss's law gives $\oint E^i dS_i=Q$ in rationalized units. Nonzero total charge excludes a source-free $G_{\mu\nu}=0$ exterior; it is electrovacuum instead. Zero net charge is not sufficient either: multipole fields or radiation can remain. For the exact vacuum target choose neutral ECD or verify all exterior nongravitational fields vanish.

**Quantum vacuum polarization.** A curved-spacetime quantum vacuum need not have $\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\rm ren}=0$. A classical/leading coarse-grained GR vacuum exterior is a declared approximation; it is not automatically an exact semiclassical QFT exterior. Renormalization, vacuum terms and their error budget must be specified.

**Decision:** A has the clearer local route to a smooth vacuum exterior in the neutral classical benchmark. B has plausible compact-support leading moments, but its complete quantum/EC boundary problem remains open. No global exterior is constructed for either.
