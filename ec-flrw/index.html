Einstein–Cartan research / Phase 1 · FLRW

Spin-fluid collapse laboratory

One stable address. Change the assumptions and investigate scale factor, Hubble rate, and e-folds of acceleration. This is a dimensionless, spatially flat FLRW baseline, not a simulation of a Kerr black hole.

Model controls

Fixed assumptions

Flat FLRW (k = 0); homogeneous isotropic effective Weyssenhoff spin fluid; p = wρ; conserved particle number; ρ ∝ a−3(1+w); spin squared ∝ a−6. No shear, spin-gradient terms, particle production, cosmological constant or Holst sector.

The bounce is normalised to a(0) = 1, H(0) = 0. Time is dimensionless; curves cannot be converted into seconds without restoring a physical density scale.

Equations used

H² = a−3(1+w) − a−6

Integrate the corresponding acceleration equation through the bounce: ä = −(1+3w)a−2−3w/2 + 2a−5. This is a specific reduced model, not the complete Einstein–Cartan tensor system.

Nacc = ln(aend/aB)
Where does Nacc come from?

The spin-fluid acceleration equation is ä/a = −κ[(1+3w)ρ − 4κS²]/6. Set ä = 0 to find the end of acceleration. Conservation gives ρ ∝ a−3(1+w), S² ∝ a−6. For a flat bounce, ρB = κS²B. Thus aend/aB = [4/(1+3w)]1/[3(1−w)] and Nacc = ln[4/(1+3w)]/[3(1−w)]. This expression is valid for −1/3 < w < 1 under these assumptions; here the UI restricts w to [0, 0.9].

Published assumptions versus our simplification

Bréchet et al. (arXiv:0706.2367) derive a more general Weyssenhoff effective stress tensor, including spin-gradient contributions. Hashemi et al. (arXiv:1407.4103) study homogeneous collapsing spin fluids, including curvature and an initially stationary configuration. Our baseline instead fixes k = 0 and normalises a bounce at t = 0. Thus its Nacc numbers are analytical predictions of this baseline, not reported results of Hashemi's collapse model.

Exact equations and the initial constraint

From Popławski, arXiv:2007.11556, equations (10), (14), (25), in the convention 8πG=1:

(Ẏ²+1)/Y² + 2ẊẎ/(XY) = ρ̃
2Ÿ/Y + (Ẏ²+1)/Y² = −p̃
Ẍ/X + Ÿ/Y + ẊẎ/(XY) = −p̃

This numerical example sets X(0)=Y(0)=1, ρ̃=ρ−S, p̃=wρ−S, with ρ=ρ₀(XY²)^−(1+w), S=S₀(XY²)^−2. The initial energy density ρ₀ is solved from the first (constraint) equation; not all initial variables can be chosen freely. The two acceleration equations are integrated; the first is monitored for consistency.

Computation stops before a scale factor reaches 0.12 or a time limit of 5 (arbitrary dimensionless units). This threshold is a numerical safeguard, not a physical bounce or singularity proof. The KS sector assumes an averaged effective perfect-fluid stress tensor; spin-gradient effects and microscopic fluid dynamics are not resolved.
