Model controls

Fixed assumptions

Flat FLRW (k = 0); homogeneous isotropic effective Weyssenhoff spin fluid; p = wρ; conserved particle number; ρ ∝ a−3(1+w); spin squared ∝ a−6. No shear, spin-gradient terms, particle production, cosmological constant or Holst sector.

The bounce is normalised to a(0) = 1, H(0) = 0. Time is dimensionless; curves cannot be converted into seconds without restoring a physical density scale.

Nacc = ln(aend/aB)
Where does Nacc come from?

The spin-fluid acceleration equation is ä/a = −κ[(1+3w)ρ − 4κS²]/6. Set ä = 0 to find the end of acceleration. Conservation gives ρ ∝ a−3(1+w), S² ∝ a−6. For a flat bounce, ρB = κS²B. Thus aend/aB = [4/(1+3w)]1/[3(1−w)] and Nacc = ln[4/(1+3w)]/[3(1−w)]. This expression is valid for −1/3 < w < 1 under these assumptions; here the UI restricts w to [0, 0.9].

Published assumptions versus our simplification

Bréchet et al. (arXiv:0706.2367) derive a more general Weyssenhoff effective stress tensor, including spin-gradient contributions. Hashemi et al. (arXiv:1407.4103) study homogeneous collapsing spin fluids, including curvature and an initially stationary configuration. Our baseline instead fixes k = 0 and normalises a bounce at t = 0. Thus its Nacc numbers are analytical predictions of this baseline, not reported results of Hashemi's collapse model.
